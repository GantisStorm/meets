Scope: The Meetings browser in the native macOS dashboard — "All Meetings" and every folder scope, including the dated sections and the follow-up threads hanging inside them, the folder level above them, and the browser header with its meeting actions and display controls.

Mode: Operate.

Audience and job: People reviewing a growing archive of recorded meetings need to find a meeting, recognize which meetings belong to the same follow-up thread, and open the right one — without the archive turning into an undifferentiated list.

Primary task: Scan the ledger for the thread that matters, open a meeting, and move, delete, or start a follow-up from the exact meeting the user is looking at.

Constraints: Preserve opening, move/create-folder, delete confirmation, follow-up creation, and status/folder context. Keep Calendar/Coming Up and active-recording behavior untouched. Native SwiftUI controls, macOS 14.2, light and dark appearances, and a detail column that can narrow to about 220 points once the sidebar is subtracted. No transcript loading for the whole library and no per-row database reads, and no per-row date-formatter allocation.

Chosen direction: One ledger, one column, no cards. The page keeps one heading — the scope name — with a single count line under it; the count reports current state only (range, and how many earlier meetings are on screen for thread context) and never explains the product, and one compact toolbar carries the meeting actions and the sort and range controls, wrapping in that order as the column narrows. Under them the archive is a ledger whose spine is time: `MeetingBrowserLogic.ledgerGroups` sorts the families by the start time of the meeting each one started from — never by how recent its follow-ups are — and runs them through `MeetingLedgerSectionKind` — Today, Yesterday, Earlier this week, Last week, then month names, with the year only when it is not this year — printing one pinned heading per run, so newest-first reads today downwards and oldest-first reads the same sections in reverse. Because the sort is on the root, a family kept on screen by a recent follow-up still files under its own root's date instead of surfacing among the recent days and splitting that date's heading in two.

Each section is a `LazyVStack` with pinned headers: a sentence-case caption in the secondary tone, a hairline rule filling the rest of the line, and the section's meeting count on the trailing edge, filled with the base canvas so rows scroll under it. Nothing is a card and nothing is nested in a card. A row is a 64-point time gutter and a body: the gutter right-aligns one monospaced-digit label — the time alone under Today and Yesterday, "Wed 9:30 AM" under the named weeks, "17 · 9:00 AM" under a month — so the ledger can be read down one edge. The body is the title at 14pt semibold, then one metadata line: status badge, "Outside range" chip, duration, the folder leaf when the meeting sits outside the folder being viewed, and 10-point glyphs for imported audio, a retained recording, and follow-ups that live in another scope. A loaded record adds one preview line. The ellipsis menu sits on the trailing edge at hover opacity but stays keyboard- and VoiceOver-reachable; the row opens on a tap gesture over its body rather than a wrapping button, so the menu and the follow-up pill keep their own clicks.

A follow-up family is a root row plus a plain sublist. While a meeting has follow-ups its metadata line ends in a pill that counts them — "1 follow-up" on its own, "7 follow-ups" for a branch — and that adds "· 6 in range" while a date range is active and the family is closed. The pill is its own button, so opening a family never opens the meeting. Opening it unfolds every follow-up in thread order, one fixed 24-point step in from the root's body, 2 points under the root, each row separated by its own row padding, and 4 points between families; folded, the root stands alone with its pill. Because that step is flat, a follow-up deeper than the first says in words which meeting it follows on from — a caption under its metadata line, "Follow-up to <title>" — where the row directly under the root needs none. The pill's chevron turns 90 degrees as the sublist slides in over 180ms ease-out. Folders in the current level stay one quiet row of compact name/count tiles under the breadcrumb.

Memorable interaction: The pill is the count. A family says what hangs under it before it is opened — "7 follow-ups", and "· 6 in range" while a range is looking for meetings inside it — so a range that matched deep inside a thread can never quietly hide it: the family that exists only because of such a match opens itself, and the one that stays closed reports how many the range is looking for on its own pill. The pill and the meeting it sits under are two different targets: the pill unfolds the sublist, the row opens the meeting, and a click on one never does the other's job.

Review evidence: An offscreen NSHostingView harness at `/private/tmp/meets-shelves-harness` renders the real production views — `MeetingLedgerView` with its section headers, rows, follow-up pills and sublists, the shared row pieces from `MeetingListItemView`, the extracted `MeetingBrowserHeader`, and the folder tiles — inside fixed window viewports (1100×800, 280×800, 220×800) scrolled like the product, with synthetic fixtures spanning today, yesterday, earlier this week, last week, two months, and a prior year, in light and dark. Scenarios: `browser` (both families closed, pills inline) and `branching` (the seven-member family under "Design system sync" opened from its pill) at 1100, `filtered` under a one-week range with the range-retained family open and "· N in range" on the closed ones, `folder` inside a folder scope with its breadcrumb, child tile, and rows that drop the folder chip they are already in, plus `narrow-220`/`narrow-280` each with and without that range. The renders confirmed the pill ends the root's metadata line at full width with its count complete ("4 follow-ups", "7 follow-ups", "4 follow-ups · 4 in range", "7 follow-ups · 6 in range"), that its chevron points right when the family is closed and down once it is open, that the opened family renders all seven follow-ups in thread order one 24-point step in from the root, that the four rows deeper than the first carry "Follow-up to <title>" beneath their metadata line while the three directly under the root carry none, and that at 220 points the pill moves to a line of its own and renders whole — no label cut mid-word, no ellipsis, nothing overlapping. The widened time column still shows "Wed 9:30 AM" and "17 · 9:00 AM" without truncation at 1100 points, and the narrow rows still stack their metadata and wrap long titles to two lines. Section order was verified from the same renders and from the fixture's own groups: the `browser` scenario prints Today, Yesterday, Earlier this week, Last week, August (2026), July (2026), August 2025 — seven sections, each once, in descending date order — with the August-rooted family, kept on screen by a follow-up from yesterday, sitting inside that single August heading rather than above the recent days, and no heading repeated. Pinned header behaviour while scrolling is present in the layout (`pinnedViews: [.sectionHeaders]`) but is not exercised by a static render, so it stays unverified here. Revised from the rail pass: the hairline rail and its ticks, the per-depth indentation, the "N more follow-ups" row and its "Show fewer follow-ups" control are gone, replaced by the follow-up pill on the root row and a flat 24-point sublist.
