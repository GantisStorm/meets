Scope: The Meetings browser in the native macOS dashboard — "All Meetings" and every folder scope, including the dated sections and the follow-up threads hanging inside them, the folder level above them, and the browser header with its meeting actions and display controls.

Mode: Operate.

Audience and job: People reviewing a growing archive of recorded meetings need to find a meeting, recognize which meetings belong to the same follow-up thread, and open the right one — without the archive turning into an undifferentiated list.

Primary task: Scan the ledger for the thread that matters, open a meeting, and move, delete, or start a follow-up from the exact meeting the user is looking at.

Constraints: Preserve opening, move/create-folder, delete confirmation, follow-up creation, and status/folder context. Keep Calendar/Coming Up and active-recording behavior untouched. Native SwiftUI controls, macOS 14.2, light and dark appearances, and a detail column that can narrow to about 220 points once the sidebar is subtracted. No transcript loading for the whole library and no per-row database reads, and no per-row date-formatter allocation.

Chosen direction: A list of meetings by day, in text and spacing only. The page keeps one heading — the scope name — and under it the scope's own navigation: in a folder scope the breadcrumb ("All Meetings › Clients") in caption size and the secondary tone, then one line of child folders as plain text buttons ("Clients 24", the count in the tertiary tone, 20 points apart, stacking into a column when the line runs out). Nothing beside the rows is filled, bordered, or badged: no folder tiles, no count line, no chips, no pills, no rules.

The archive is a ledger of follow-up families. `MeetingBrowserLogic.ledgerGroups` stable-sorts the shelves by the start time of the meeting each family started from — never by how recent its follow-ups are — and cuts that list into sections (Today, Yesterday, Earlier this week, Last week, then month names with the year only when it is not this year), so a section is one consecutive run of families and its heading prints once. A family is its root row and, once that root's fold is open, every follow-up in thread order directly beneath it — siblings chronological, depth first — each indented one flat 20-point step past the time gutter whatever its depth, so a long thread stays a list rather than a staircase and the time column keeps one position for roots and children alike.

A root with follow-ups puts a 10-point `chevron.right` in a 20-point slot left of the gutter — every row reserves that slot, so the gutters and the titles stay aligned down the column — and the count the fold holds ("4 follow-ups", "1 follow-up") sits after the title in the tertiary tone at caption size, saying "· 2 in range" while a date range is active, the fold is closed, and some of its follow-ups are inside that range. A follow-up's gutter reads against its root rather than against the section: the time alone on the root's own day, "Sep 18" or "Sep 2025" otherwise. A scoped root whose predecessor lives outside the folder is a root like any other: it keeps its arrow and opens normally. Hover remains the only surface, the hover fill and the chevron's tertiary-to-primary turn the only states.

Memorable interaction: The chevron is the family's fold and nothing else. A root says what hangs under it before it is opened — "7 follow-ups", and "· 6 in range" while a range is looking for meetings inside it — so a range that matched deep inside a thread can never quietly hide it: the family that exists only because of such a match opens itself, and the one that stays closed reports on its own count how many the range is looking for behind it. The chevron and the meeting it sits beside are two different targets: the chevron unfolds the follow-ups, the row opens the meeting, and a click on one never does the other's job. The fold and the rows it reveals move as one thing, 180ms ease-out.

Review evidence: An offscreen NSHostingView harness at `/private/tmp/meets-shelves-harness` renders the real production views — `MeetingLedgerView` with its section headers, root rows, fold chevrons, counts and opened follow-up rows, the shared row pieces from `MeetingListItemView`, the extracted `MeetingBrowserHeader` — inside fixed window viewports (1100×800, 280×800, 220×800) scrolled like the product, with synthetic fixtures spanning today, yesterday, earlier this week, last week, two months, and a prior year, in light and dark. Scenarios: `browser` at 1100 with every family closed, `expanded` with the seven-follow-up family opened the way a click opens it, `filtered` under a one-week range, `folder` inside a folder scope with its breadcrumb and child line, and `narrow-280`/`narrow-220`, each in both appearances. `browser-light` shows the chevron in the slot left of the time column on exactly the rows that have follow-ups, the counts "4 follow-ups", "7 follow-ups" and "1 follow-up" after their titles in the tertiary tone, and titles on one line whether or not the row carries a chevron — the reserved slot is what keeps them aligned. `expanded-dark` shows `Design system sync` open with its seven descendants in thread order ("Follow-up: density review", "Follow-up: iconography audit", "Nested: focus order", "Deep: rollout dependencies with the platform team" with its processing status, "Follow-up: rollout owners", "Nested: voiceover labels", "Follow-up: motion pass"), each carrying the arrow before its title and no chevron of its own.

Geometry was measured on the pixels rather than read off the render: the vision model's readings were not trusted for position. A scan of `expanded-dark` shows every row's gutter text ending on the same x (179) while the follow-up bodies start 20 points right of their root's (215 against 194 for a root title), so the step is the body's and never the time column's. The same render was dumped through `shelf.descendants` to confirm the painted order matches the model exactly, including `Deep:` at depth 3 sitting above the `Follow-up: rollout owners` it precedes at depth 4. `filtered-light` shows the counts "4 follow-ups · 4 in range" and "7 follow-ups · 6 in range" while the range-retained family opens itself and its child reads "Follow-up: research panel scope" under a root the range dimmed. `narrow-220-light` holds everything inside the window — ink spans x 16…202 in a 220-point viewport, touching neither edge — and there the count wraps rather than truncating, which is the one thing allowed to break the row's single line.

Hover fill, the tertiary-to-primary transitions, the chevrons' rotation, the unfold animation, the menus themselves, and pinned-header behaviour while scrolling are present in the layout but are not exercised by a static render, so they stay unverified here. Revised from the flat-ledger pass: `flatRows`, the flat date-sorted column, and the follow-up-as-its-own-row reading are gone; a family is one root with its follow-ups folded under it.
