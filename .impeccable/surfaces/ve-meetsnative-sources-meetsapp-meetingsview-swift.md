Scope: The Meetings browser in the native macOS dashboard — "All Meetings" and every folder scope, including the dated sections and the follow-up threads hanging inside them, the folder level above them, and the browser header with its meeting actions and display controls.

Mode: Operate.

Audience and job: People reviewing a growing archive of recorded meetings need to find a meeting, recognize which meetings belong to the same follow-up thread, and open the right one — without the archive turning into an undifferentiated list.

Primary task: Scan the ledger for the thread that matters, open a meeting, and move, delete, or start a follow-up from the exact meeting the user is looking at.

Constraints: Preserve opening, move/create-folder, delete confirmation, follow-up creation, and status/folder context. Keep Calendar/Coming Up and active-recording behavior untouched. Native SwiftUI controls, macOS 14.2, light and dark appearances, and a detail column that can narrow to about 220 points once the sidebar is subtracted. No transcript loading for the whole library and no per-row database reads, and no per-row date-formatter allocation.

Chosen direction: One ledger, one column, no cards. The page keeps one heading — the scope name — with a single count line under it; the count reports current state only (range, and how many earlier meetings are on screen for thread context) and never explains the product, and one compact toolbar carries the meeting actions and the sort and range controls, wrapping in that order as the column narrows. Under them the archive is a ledger whose spine is time: `MeetingBrowserLogic.ledgerGroups` runs the already-sorted shelves through `MeetingLedgerSectionKind` — Today, Yesterday, Earlier this week, Last week, then month names, with the year only when it is not this year — and prints one pinned heading per run, in the order the shelves arrived, so newest-first reads today downwards and oldest-first reads the same sections in reverse instead of overriding the sort the user chose.

Each section is a `LazyVStack` with pinned headers: a sentence-case caption in the secondary tone, a hairline rule filling the rest of the line, and the section's meeting count on the trailing edge, filled with the base canvas so rows scroll under it. Nothing is a card and nothing is nested in a card. A row is a 64-point time gutter and a body: the gutter right-aligns one monospaced-digit label — the time alone under Today and Yesterday, "Wed 9:30 AM" under the named weeks, "17 · 9:00 AM" under a month — so the ledger can be read down one edge. The body is the title at 14pt semibold, then one metadata line: status badge, "Outside range" chip, duration, the folder leaf when the meeting sits outside the folder being viewed, and 10-point glyphs for imported audio, a retained recording, and follow-ups that live in another scope. A loaded record adds one preview line. The ellipsis menu sits on the trailing edge at hover opacity but stays keyboard- and VoiceOver-reachable; the whole row is the open target.

A follow-up family is a root row plus a thread hanging from a rail: a 1pt vertical path in the border tone, drawn from the root to the measured centre of the last visible follow-up, with a 10-point tick at each row's first baseline that lands exactly on that row's leading edge — both offsets come from `MeetingLedgerMetrics`, so the rail lines up whatever the depth. A folded thread shows the first two follow-ups and ends in a plain control in the rail's column: "2 more follow-ups", or "5 more follow-ups · 4 in range" while a date range is looking for meetings inside the fold, and "Show fewer follow-ups" once open. Expanding slides the rest in along the rail in 180ms ease-out. Folders in the current level stay one quiet row of compact name/count tiles under the breadcrumb.

Memorable interaction: The rail is the thread. A family's follow-ups hang from a hairline that lands on each row it reaches, and the fold at the end of that rail reports what it is holding back — so a range that matched deep inside a thread can never quietly hide it: the thread that only exists because of a match opens itself, and the one that stays folded says "· N in range" on the rail itself.

Review evidence: An offscreen NSHostingView harness at `/private/tmp/meets-shelves-harness` renders the real production views — `MeetingLedgerView` with its section headers, rows, rail and fold control, the shared row pieces from `MeetingListItemView`, the extracted `MeetingBrowserHeader`, and the folder tiles — inside fixed window viewports (1100×800, 280×800, 220×800) scrolled like the product, with synthetic fixtures spanning today, yesterday, earlier this week, last week, two months, and a prior year, in light and dark. Scenarios: `browser` (collapsed family with a "2 more follow-ups" row) and `branching` (the seven-member thread expanded, including a deep row that names its parent) at 1100, `filtered` under a one-week range with a retained thread open and "· N in range" on the folded ones, `folder` inside a folder scope with its breadcrumb, child tile, and rows that drop the folder chip they are already in, plus `narrow-220`/`narrow-280` each with and without that range. The renders confirmed the rail and its ticks land on each follow-up's leading edge at full width and at 220 points, that the folded control sits in the follow-up column, that the widened time column shows "Wed 9:30 AM" and "17 · 9:00 AM" without truncation at 1100 points, and that at 220 points the row stacks its metadata into two lines with nothing clipped or overlapping. Pinned header behaviour while scrolling is present in the layout (`pinnedViews: [.sectionHeaders]`) but is not exercised by a static render, so it stays unverified here. Revised from the card pass: the bordered shelf cards, the inset follow-up panel, the chevron disclosure row, and the "Expand follow-ups · N in range" label are gone, replaced by date sections, a fixed time gutter, the thread rail, and the fold count.
